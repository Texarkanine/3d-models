# Product Context

## Target Audience

- People who keep a fire extinguisher in a car, especially a race car or an off-road vehicle.
- The operator first. Strange noises in the car mean something is wrong, and they want to find the cause rather than learn to ignore it.

## Use Cases

- Carry a full-size extinguisher in the car without the handle, trigger, or lock rattling as the car moves.
- Use the extinguisher in a fire without taking the printed part off first.

## Key Benefits

- The handle, trigger, and lock on a typical extinguisher are light aluminium or plastic with a lot of free play. At the frequencies a car runs at, those loose parts vibrate and make noise. This part slips onto the business end of the handle, holds those pieces still, and gives them soft plastic to touch instead of each other.
- It is flexible. In a fire it deforms out of the way of the trigger, so it does not have to be removed to put the fire out.

## Success Criteria

- Printed in TPU 95, it slides onto the handle of the operator's 2.5 pound and 5 pound extinguishers and those handles stop rattling on the road.
- The extinguisher can still be operated with the part in place.
- The geometry in this directory is the one that was printed on 2025-03-30. Git holds the versions. The filenames do not.

## Key Constraints

- Print in TPU 95: firm, and flexible enough to slide on and to move out of the way of the trigger.
- Known to fit the operator's 2.5 pound and 5 pound extinguishers. Not tried on the small half-pound and 2 pound bottles, or on a 10, 15, or 20 pound restaurant extinguisher.
- Photos of the print are the files in `img/`. Those files are the listing photos.
