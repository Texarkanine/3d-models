# Product Context

## Target Audience

- People who carry a jerry can in a metal cage and want the can off the bare frame.
- The operator first. The lengths that worked are the ones on their own cage.

## Use Cases

- Slip a soft bumper onto a cage rail so the can is cushioned and metal does not touch metal.
- Stretch the 1 inch piece along its length to match a rail. A longer piece can be cut down. Shorter pieces can be set end to end on one rail.

## Key Benefits

- The can sits on plastic instead of the frame.
- One shape covers different rail lengths, because the piece is meant to be stretched.

## Success Criteria

- It slips onto a cage whose rails are 1 inch high and 3/32 inch thick, and it stays there in use.
- The print on the operator's cage, shown in `img/`, does that.

## Key Constraints

- Some give on thickness. Little give on height. A rail much taller than 1 inch will not enter.
- Print it in TPU 90 or softer. TPU 95 is stiff to fit on and can crack at a bend.
