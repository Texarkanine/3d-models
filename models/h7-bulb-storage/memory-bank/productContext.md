# Product Context

## Target Audience

- People who keep spare H7 headlamp bulbs, especially LED replacements with a heatsink, fan or cable on the back, and who own a home FDM 3D printer.
- The operator first. The design will be shared publicly, so it must fit any bulb that meets the H7 standard, not only the operator's bulbs.

## Use Cases

- Store a spare H7 bulb in a glovebox, bag, bin or car without harming the head (glass or LEDs).
- Put a bulb in and take it out by hand, many times. Fingers, needle-nose pliers or a small screwdriver are acceptable tools.
- Pack several covers side by side in a box, where they stay put.
- Fit a non-standard bulb by measuring it and changing a few dimensions before printing.

## Key Benefits

- Holds the bulb by its flange, the way a headlamp does. The head is enclosed; the back of the bulb (heatsink, fan, cable) stays free, whatever its size.
- The bulb sits at one rotation, keyed to its tab, and cannot turn.
- The clip latches with a click and does not shake loose, yet comes out without a fight.
- Prints on a home printer without supports that are hard to remove.

## Success Criteria

- Fits every bulb within the H7 cap standard (PX26d), from its smallest to its largest sizes, plus clearance; confirmed by test prints on real bulbs.
- One clip choice per flange-thickness band latches firmly and releases cleanly.
- Prints in one obvious orientation with no supports, or only easy-break supports on faces that do no work.
- Files open in a slicer (STL) and in Solid Edge Community Edition (STEP).

## Key Constraints

- Build to the published standard plus clearance. The operator's own bulbs are data points; a non-standard bulb is an exception handled by changing dimensions, not by changing the defaults.
- Faces that locate or hold the bulb or the clip must print without supports and without sagging. Cosmetic faces may take supports if they break off easily.
- Small enough to store compactly: no longer than the longest bulb the standard allows, plus about a centimetre of spare.
- Instructions are for home-printer users: plain language, no trade jargon.
- Everything in the repository may become public, so it must be shareable.
